What is this plan for?

The Plan at a Glance

This plan turns MFM's business direction into marketing action: who MFM targets, what it says, which offers open the door, which channels carry the message, and how interest becomes signed work.

Working campaign lineBrief once.
We manage the rest.

One line that explains the managed model in four words: the client briefs MFM once, and MFM plans, coordinates and delivers through its network of specialists.

Business goalWin clients for managed marketing and PR, and grow recurring contracts.
Marketing goalMake MFM known and trusted as the one accountable partner for marketing and communication needs.
Primary audienceMarketing and communication decision-makers in established Egyptian companies and institutions.
Core messageOne partner instead of many suppliers. Planned, coordinated and delivered by MFM.
Main channelsLinkedIn (MFM and the Founder), direct B2B relationships, PR Circle events, then the website.
TimeframeThree waves, starting December 2026. December and January are detailed in the content strategy.
Wave 1 · Dec – JanBe seen. Be clear.Explain the model, build presence.Launch MFM's LinkedIn presence, the Founder's voice, the first lead magnets and the target-account list. Organic first.
Wave 2 · Ramadan seasonBe useful.Help clients deliver their season.Ramadan is expected to begin around 8 February 2027. Brands plan in December and January; MFM shows up as the partner that gets it done.
Wave 3 · After readinessActivate.Scale what works.Once the website, offers and execution network are ready: outreach at scale, case studies, paid amplification and referrals.
02
What is true today?

Where We Start

The plan starts from MFM's real position, so the first months focus on presence and clarity before scale.

What we have
  • Experience in Marketing Facility Management and PR.
  • A managed model clients can understand quickly: one partner, many specialists.
  • An execution network of freelancers, agencies, production and media providers.
  • PR Circle's professional community and Gen Z Pulse's youth platform, with an existing campaign.
  • A Founder with long experience and relationships in the field.
What is missing
  • An active MFM presence on LinkedIn and other channels.
  • A website that explains the managed model and captures inquiries.
  • Documented case studies that clients have approved.
  • Clear entry offers that make the first conversation easy.
  • A simple system to track leads, meetings and proposals.
What this means for the plan

Wave 1 builds presence and trust with content, relationships and lead magnets that do not depend on the new website. Paid promotion and large-scale outreach wait until the website and operational readiness are in place.

To confirm with MFM

Account access for any existing MFM, PR Circle and Gen Z Pulse channels, and the current status of the Gen Z Pulse campaign.

04
Who exactly are we talking to?

Target Audiences

Priority 1The stretched marketing team

Established companies with a marketing or communications manager and a small team, juggling several suppliers.

  • Need: get campaigns, content, events and PR done without managing ten vendors
  • Trigger: a launch, a season (Ramadan), a new year plan
  • Decision-maker: marketing manager, head of communications, GM
Priority 2The institution

Institutions and organizations that need communication support, forums and public relations.

  • Need: reliable communication and event delivery with one accountable partner
  • Trigger: a conference, a report, a public announcement
  • Decision-maker: head of communications, board
Priority 3The youth-seeking brand

Brands that want to reach young audiences, through Gen Z Pulse partnerships and MFM's managed services.

  • Need: credible access to young Egyptians
  • Trigger: youth campaigns, graduate hiring, campus presence
  • Decision-maker: brand manager, employer-brand or HR lead

How a buyer moves

StageWhat they thinkWhat MFM gives them
Notice"Managing all these suppliers is exhausting."Relatable content: the supplier problem, named honestly
Understand"So one partner can handle all of it?"The "Brief once" story; how a brief becomes a campaign
Trust"Do they actually deliver?"The Founder's experience, team faces, approved case studies, PR Circle standing
Try"Let's see if they're useful."A free lead magnet, then a marketing-needs conversation
Buy"Send us a proposal."A clear scope: project, PR retainer or managed marketing retainer
Stay and refer"This made my life easier."Reliable delivery, a quarterly review, a simple referral ask
05
What does MFM say, and to whom?

Positioning & Messaging

Working positioning · to be confirmed with MFM's leadershipMFM is a Marketing Facility Management and Public Relations partner that helps businesses plan, coordinate, and deliver integrated marketing and communication services through a managed network of specialized execution partners.

Message house

Pillar 1One partner

One brief, one point of contact, one accountable team, instead of a separate supplier for every service.

Pillar 2The right specialists

A managed network of specialists, chosen for each project and coordinated by MFM.

Pillar 3PR that builds reputation

Public relations and communications expertise, backed by the Founder's experience and PR Circle's community.

AudienceKey messageProof to show
Marketing managers"Brief once. We manage the rest."The process, the crew behind a campaign, delivery timelines
Institutions"One accountable partner for communications and events."PR expertise, PR Circle, the Founder's standing
Youth-seeking brands"Reach young Egyptians with people who know them."Gen Z Pulse community and concept
Execution partners"Work on good briefs with a reliable partner."Clear briefs, fair process, steady work
Words to use
  • Brief, managed, delivered, one partner, specialists
  • Plain business language; specific outcomes
  • Arabic and English, matched to the channel
Words to avoid
  • "360", "next level", "solutions" without saying what
  • Claims without proof (clients, numbers, awards)
  • Promising services MFM has not confirmed

What makes the first conversation easy?

Offers & Entry Points

People rarely buy a retainer from a post. They take a small, useful step first. The plan uses free entry points that lead to a conversation, then to one of MFM's core offers.

Free entry points (lead magnets)

Dec – JanRamadan Readiness Checklist

A one-page checklist: what to brief, book and produce, week by week, before Ramadan.

Keyword: RAMADAN

JanuaryThe One-Page Marketing Brief

A simple template to brief any campaign clearly, the same brief MFM asks clients for.

Keyword: BRIEF

OngoingMarketing Needs Conversation

A 30-minute call to map the client's marketing needs for the season and who should deliver each one.

Keyword: CALL

Core offers the conversation leads to

OfferBest forWhy it matters
Managed Marketing RetainerCompanies with continuous marketing needsRecurring revenue; MFM becomes the marketing back office
PR RetainerOrganizations needing ongoing communicationsRecurring revenue built on MFM's PR strength
Project-Based ServicesA launch, an event, a seasonLow-risk first project that can become a retainer
Specialized Service PackagesRecurring needs like seasonal campaignsEasy to buy; e.g. a Ramadan campaign package
Corporate PartnershipsLarger companies and institutionsLong-term relationships across services
To confirm with MFM

The exact contents and scope of each package before it is promoted publicly. Lead magnets can launch first because they do not depend on pricing.

The big idea

Every client brief hides a dozen suppliers. MFM's marketing makes that problem visible, and then makes it disappear.

How does interest become signed work?

Business Development System

AttractContent, Founder posts, PR Circle and Gen Z Pulse activityReach · followers
CaptureLead magnets by DM keyword, WhatsApp or emailDownloads · replies
ConverseMarketing Needs Conversation; invitations to gatheringsCalls · meetings
ProposeClear scope: project, PR retainer or managed retainerProposals
DeliverManaged delivery through the execution networkProjects won
ReferQuarterly review, thank-you, referral askReferrals
Target accountsA list of 50

50 companies that match Priority 1 and 2, with the decision-maker's name and how we are connected. The Founder and MFM warm them up on LinkedIn before any invitation.

Invite, don't pitchValue before the ask

Each target receives something useful (a checklist, an article, an invitation to a conversation) before any proposal. Ten personal contacts a week is enough to start.

PipelineOne simple tracker

A shared sheet with every lead: source, stage, next step, date. Reviewed every week. No lead is left without a next step.

Response rulesFast and human

Replies to DMs and WhatsApp the same working day. Lead magnets delivered within an hour. A follow-up after three working days.
